The main comparison reconstructs scenes under supplied camera and actor states. The last four frames evaluate conditional temporal extrapolation. Table~\ref{tab:shared-inputs} lists test conditions. Target human joints come from independent offline annotation/fitting, and human shape and binding are recovered from context. Test LiDAR is reserved for evaluation. All test-time network weights remain fixed.

\paragraph{Training resources.}
We retrain STORM, DGGT, and StreetForward from random initialization, preserving their native architectures. These three baselines and Ours use the same real training logs $D_R$, synthetic scenes $D_S$, and complete assets $D_A$. Baselines receive RGB, depth, silhouette, and complete-asset target views through their native objectives. Ours additionally uses cell-empty and template-binding supervision. Checkpoints are selected on the shared development set within each fixed training budget (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-resources}). Matching recovery, codec, data, and optimization in the CCM ablation isolates the contribution of component exchange.



\paragraph{Full generative pipelines.}
DGGT-G denotes the Gaussian branch of DGGT; DGGT-Full retains its diffusion refinement. STORM+Difix applies DIFIX3D+~\cite{wu2025difix3d} to STORM renders. Main Table 2 compares these complete outputs at identical offsets and reference populations. Table~\ref{tab:training-cost-statistics} gives training/adaptation costs, excluding historical pretraining of frozen foundation models.

\paragraph{Shared state adapter.}
The baselines use a common deterministic state adapter. Visible Gaussian rendering contributions are projected into the same context instance masks and accumulate weighted identity votes. Vehicles are canonicalized with the inverse supplied root SE(3), transforming centers, covariances, and directional coordinate bases together. Human Gaussians obtain 17-joint skinning weights by barycentric interpolation on corresponding triangles of the context-recovered identity template, followed by inverse skinning. The adapter preserves predicted appearance and adds no prediction network. Unbound outputs remain scored, and actor edits that cannot be assembled count as failures.

For the same Gaussian set, inverse and forward placement produce a maximum linear-RGB difference below $4\times10^{-6}$. Converting a native time-varying representation into fixed canonical assets changes cross-time reconstruction as shown in Table~\ref{tab:adapter-effect}. The adapted column is used in the main comparison.





The first 200 valid synchronized frames per test sequence form ten non-overlapping 20-frame clips, totaling 2,020 clips. Context indices are $\{0,5,10,15\}$ for three cameras. Twelve interpolation frames yield 72,720 images, and the four extrapolation frames $\{16,17,18,19\}$ yield 24,240, totaling 96,960 targets. Every target timestamp has valid dynamic masks and depth-evaluation samples. Group-specific metrics in Table~\ref{tab:temporal-groups} use $3{:}1$ interpolation/extrapolation weights; full-image SSIM is reported separately as the method-level aggregate from main Table 1. Depth scale alignment is used only for evaluation, with no feedback to reconstruction or rendering.



\paragraph{Depth scale and metric evaluation.}
The aligned depth columns use a positive per-frame scale on a fixed valid LiDAR mask $M_t$,
\begin{equation}
s_t=\operatorname{median}_{p\in M_t}\frac{d_t(p)}{\widehat d_t(p)}.
\label{eq:depth-alignment}
\end{equation}
Only scale is fitted. Table~\ref{tab:depth-scale} uses the same masks and frame--clip--sequence aggregation for aligned and unaligned depth. Alignment is applied inside the depth evaluator; reconstruction, composition, surface distances, and RGB reprojection use the original metric coordinates.

\paragraph{Predicted surfaces and geometry metrics.}
We construct surfaces by rendering and fusion. Gaussians with opacity at least 0.01 are evaluated within $3\sigma$. Six axis-aligned orthographic views are rendered in blocks over a common evaluation domain, with pixel size at most 1 mm inside each block. A ray's first accumulated-opacity crossing of 0.5 defines a surface depth; rays below this opacity have no predicted surface. We fuse depths into a sparse TSDF with 2 mm voxels and an 8 mm truncation distance, and extract the mesh in valid, supported voxels, leaving unknown regions open. Native neural fields use the same accumulated-opacity threshold, fusion, and distance evaluation.

Predicted and reference meshes each supply 65,536 area-uniform samples. A BVH computes each sample's nearest distance to the other triangle mesh. Coordinates are in meters and distances are non-squared Euclidean distances. Evaluation keeps the original metric scale and alignment. With predicted samples $P$, reference samples $G$, and corresponding surfaces $S_{\rm pred}$ and $S_{\rm GT}$ in the evaluation region,
\begin{align}
P_\tau&=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{p\in P}\mathbf1[d(p,S_{\rm GT})<\tau],\\
R_\tau&=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf1[d(g,S_{\rm pred})<\tau],\\
F_\tau&=\frac{2P_\tau R_\tau}{P_\tau+R_\tau}.
\label{eq:geometry-metrics}
\end{align}
Coverage is reference-surface recall $R_\tau$. We compute metrics per scene and seed before averaging. Asset coverage uses only surfaces assigned to that actor, with 2 cm vehicle and 1 cm human thresholds. Complete reference geometry and input visibility define newly visible surfaces; reference surfaces outside query support remain in the coverage denominator.

\paragraph{Real-view correspondences.}
Independent reference geometry establishes correspondences between recorded and off-trajectory views. Dynamic actors are transformed with shared states. The reference evaluator removes occlusion-ambiguous correspondences for every method; finite outputs and rendering coverage determine method validity. Test LiDAR is used only by the evaluator. Linear-RGB MAE is measured on both each method's valid subset and their intersection, which covers 58.7\% of the reference set (Table~\ref{tab:reprojection}). CCM reduces common-support MAE from 0.028 to 0.019 and raises overall coverage from 86.9\% to 92.4\% relative to w/o CCM.

\paragraph{Task-adapted completion mechanisms.}
The scene-latent control adapts the scene-level Gaussian latent generation mechanism of CompleteSplat~\cite{liao2025complete}; the object-coupling control adapts JRM's cross-object latent coupling~\cite{wu2026jrm}. Both operate through the shared recovery module, codec, query layout, and state interface. They replace the completion interaction mechanism while using the same input evidence, training data, supervision, and 24-step sampling setup. The shared recovery and codec stay fixed. The completion training schedule is given in Appendix B, and comparison scores average three training and three generation seeds without sample selection.

Table~\ref{tab:hidden-regions} uses the same 100 synthetic test scenes and 12,000 shifted target images per seed combination. Each learned variant produces 108,000 outputs across nine seed combinations; scene-wise scores include all views, frames, and seeds. Over the object-coupling control, CCM adds 0.67 dB PSNR, reduces LPIPS by 0.020, and increases coverage and F-score by 2.6 and 3.6 percentage points. The PSNR gain over the scene-latent control is 0.94 dB. Surface evaluation uses original metric coordinates, 65,536 area-uniform samples from each mesh, and a shared 2 cm point-to-triangle threshold.






\paragraph{Visibility-stratified synthetic evaluation.}
Table~\ref{tab:visibility} reuses the same 100 synthetic test scenes, $\pm2$ m shifts, inputs, checkpoints, and generated samples as the paired hidden-region evaluation. Each scene supplies one 20-frame clip with three cameras and context timestamps $\{0,5,10,15\}$ (12 images). Targets cover both shift directions, all 20 timestamps, and three cameras, giving 12,000 images per seed combination. Context timestamps are also valid targets because the shifted cameras differ from the input cameras. Three training seeds and three generation seeds reuse trained weights without adapting on these scenes.

Visibility $v$ is the fraction of a fixed reference surface region's area covered by the union of input views. Well-observed regions have coverage $\geq80\%$, severely missing regions $\leq20\%$, and intermediate regions $20\%<v<80\%$. Reference-surface correspondences assign target pixels to background, vehicle, or human and to a visibility group. Methods use the same valid masks; group-wise frame means are averaged with equal scene weight. Group PSNRs and full-image PSNR are computed independently. The hidden-region test requires exactly 0\% input coverage, whereas the severely missing group permits up to 20\%.

CCM adds 1.02, 1.27, and 1.53 dB for severely missing background, vehicle, and human regions (Table~\ref{tab:visibility}). Intermediate regions gain 0.58, 0.72, and 0.84 dB; well-observed regions gain 0.04, 0.05, and 0.03 dB. Benefits increase as input coverage decreases.

\paragraph{Real-scene edit ratings.}
Training seed 0 and generation seed 7 give forty edits (Table~\ref{tab:edit-failures}). Ours succeeds in all twenty vehicle rotations and seventeen human edits. Its three human failures involve a large elbow bend, leg self-occlusion, and loose-clothing boundaries. Vehicle dimensions and human shape parameters remain fixed, and previously observed human texture has canonical linear-RGB MAE 0.014.



\paragraph{Seed variation and scene clustering.}
The three training seeds $\{0,1,2\}$ and three generation seeds $\{7,17,27\}$ yield 360 edits on the same twenty real scenes (Table~\ref{tab:edit-seeds}). Scene-cluster bootstrap sampling retains all eighteen edits for a selected scene, giving a 95\% structural-integrity interval of 85.28--99.17\%. The 26 failures are concentrated in four scenes with 9, 8, 6, and 3 failures.



\paragraph{Newly visible texture, identity, and geometry.}
An asset surface is newly visible when it is invisible in every input observation and visible after editing. Canonical surface correspondences and visibility tests define these regions. Five independent raters inspect newly visible texture, appearance identity, and local geometry; each dimension passes with at least four positive judgments. Texture ratings inspect blur, repetition, stretching, and seams. Identity ratings inspect vehicle shape and paint, and human clothing, body shape, and visible identity features. Geometry ratings inspect wheel/body connections, limb volume, joint bends, and intersections.

Table~\ref{tab:real-edit-quality} scores 360 actor edits from twenty scenes and nine seed combinations; camera shifts add no actor-edit trials. Joint acceptance is the per-edit intersection of the four criteria, yielding 298 passes (82.78\%). The 62 failures comprise 26 structural failures and 36 structurally intact edits that fail at least one other quality check.
