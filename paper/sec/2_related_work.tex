\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Compositional reconstruction and simulation.}
Street Gaussians represents the background and tracked vehicles as separate Gaussian components~\cite{yan2024street}. OmniRe uses category-dependent canonical representations to include both rigid and articulated actors~\cite{chen2025omnire}. HUGSIM combines reconstructed content with interactive state updates and a simulation backend~\cite{zhou2024hugsim}. These works establish content/state separation and category-specific motion models. DriveCompose uses the same separation to define what completion should produce: a fixed background and canonical assets whose identities, local geometry, and binding persist across trajectories. The completion variables span these components jointly, rather than requiring their unobserved surfaces to be generated independently.

\paragraph{Feed-forward driving reconstruction.}
STORM, DGGT, StreetForward, and PointForward recover driving-scene representations from temporal observations~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward,chi2026pointforward}. We use temporal recovery to establish the evidence that conditions completion: the front end predicts geometry, identity, and state, after which a fixed query layout records surface support, free space, and unknown regions. Conditional flow operates on the unknown codes during offline construction. Subsequent rendering reads the accepted content and current state, without repeating latent completion for each trajectory.

\paragraph{Generative scene and asset completion.}
StreetCrafter conditions video diffusion on LiDAR renderings and uses generated views to guide Gaussian reconstruction~\cite{streetcrafter}; HorizonForge combines editable Gaussian--mesh content with video diffusion~\cite{wang2026horizonforge}. WorldSplat generates Gaussians followed by rendered-video refinement~\cite{zhu2025worldsplat}. DriveCompose instead completes the content used directly by its state-driven Gaussian renderer. Among latent methods, GenAssets targets driving assets, CompleteSplat targets Gaussian scene completion, and JRM aggregates unaligned observations of a shared object through latent flow matching~\cite{yang2025genassets,liao2025complete,wu2026jrm}. LatentWorld provides entity-centered control over semantic occupancy generation~\cite{park2026latentworld}. These methods use different generation units: an asset, a scene, or a shared object reconstructed from several observations. In DriveCompose, these units coexist in one query layout, retaining category-specific transforms and sharing an observed-view check. Our variables couple world-space background with rigid and articulated canonical assets. Observed/free constraints govern sampling and decoding, and the acceptance check controls how completed components enter one reusable RGB-rendering world.
