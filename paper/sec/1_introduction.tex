\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Driving simulation and scene editing require 3D scenes that support changes in camera viewpoint, vehicle motion, and human pose~\cite{yang2023unisim,zhou2024hugsim}. However, driving images reveal only the surfaces visible from the recorded viewpoints and actor configurations~\cite{sun2020waymo,caesar2020nuscenes}. Moving a camera, turning a vehicle, or changing a pedestrian's pose exposes previously unobserved regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). Reconstructing scenes for these operations therefore requires completing the missing geometry and appearance while ensuring that completed vehicle and human surfaces move with their respective actors.

Recent feed-forward methods fuse multiple images to directly predict driving-scene geometry and Gaussian representations~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward,chi2026pointforward}. Motion estimation and visibility modeling further help align dynamic objects and integrate observations over time. Multi-frame aggregation increases coverage of surfaces visible in the input sequence. When a new viewpoint or actor motion reveals surfaces unseen throughout the sequence, reconstruction must infer their geometry and appearance.

Generative reconstruction uses asset priors, Gaussian completion, or generated views to infer unobserved content~\cite{yang2025genassets,liao2025complete,streetcrafter,zhu2025worldsplat}. Meanwhile, compositional reconstruction represents the background and moving actors separately to support vehicle motion and human pose control~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire,zhou2024hugsim}. Applying generative completion to such scenes requires generated surfaces to move with their actors. When an actor is only partially observed, information from the background and other actors can provide additional context for inferring its missing content.

To this end, we introduce DriveCompose, which reconstructs driving image sequences into a generatively completed 3D scene with independently controllable actors. We represent the background in world coordinates, rigid surfaces in each asset's own coordinate frame, and human surfaces in a reference pose with skeletal bindings~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire}. Completion operates directly on these representations, allowing generated rigid and human surfaces to follow their actors' motion and pose changes (Sec.~\ref{sec:representation}). The model jointly completes missing background and actor content using shared scene context.

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} follows the reconstruction pipeline. A visual encoder extracts image features and estimates scene geometry and actor motion. Motion-aware aggregation aligns features across views and time in the background and canonical asset frames, and type-specific encoders map them into a shared latent dimension. Local attention captures structure within each component. Cross-component memory gathers background and actor features and returns the shared scene context to each component, allowing missing surfaces to be inferred from both local observations and their surroundings. The model learns this joint completion through flow matching~\cite{lipman2022flowmatching} while retaining observed latent codes.

Type-specific Gaussian decoders turn the completed codes into a background, canonical rigid assets, and articulated humans~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}. Rigid geometry follows asset poses, and human geometry follows skeletal motion through its skinning weights. Scene composition places these components in world coordinates for the requested camera and actor states, reusing the same completed content across state changes (Sec.~\ref{sec:composition}).

On Waymo, DriveCompose improves dynamic scene reconstruction and off-trajectory view synthesis over the evaluated baselines (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce DriveCompose, a generative reconstruction framework for closed-loop driving simulation. It recovers and completes backgrounds, vehicles, and humans from image sequences to construct compositional Gaussian scene assets. The resulting scene reuses the same completed assets for composition and rendering as camera poses, vehicle poses, and human poses change.
\item We develop a cross-component latent completion model based on conditional flow matching. Local attention models spatial relationships within each component, while cross-component memory aggregates background and actor features to provide scene context for generating each component's missing content.
\item We evaluate the framework and its key mechanisms through dynamic reconstruction, off-trajectory view synthesis, and component ablations. Experiments show that DriveCompose outperforms the evaluated baselines on reconstruction and view synthesis.
\end{itemize}
