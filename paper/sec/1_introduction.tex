\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A driving log records the surfaces visible along one trajectory. Moving a simulated camera can expose a road patch hidden behind a vehicle; turning that vehicle reveals its unseen side; changing a pedestrian's pose exposes a different part of the body. A world built for such interventions must explain the recorded images and provide content for surfaces those images do not cover. The completed content must also remain attached to the same scene and actor identities as their states change.

Structured Gaussian representations provide a useful basis for this task. Street Gaussians separates tracked vehicles from the background, and OmniRe extends canonical modeling to articulated humans and other actor types~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire}. HUGSIM connects reconstructed scenes to control-driven state updates~\cite{zhou2024hugsim}. These representations distinguish persistent content from motion: a vehicle has one local shape, and a human has one canonical surface and skeleton binding. Completion must respect this distinction. Geometry generated for a particular view or pose must still have a well-defined place in the reusable world.

Generative methods address missing observations at several levels. StreetCrafter and HorizonForge combine three-dimensional conditions with video diffusion, while WorldSplat generates Gaussians and refines their rendered videos~\cite{streetcrafter,wang2026horizonforge,zhu2025worldsplat}. GenAssets learns a generative latent space for driving assets, and CompleteSplat completes Gaussian scenes from partial image evidence~\cite{yang2025genassets,liao2025complete}. A driving world brings these completion problems together. Its background is represented in world coordinates, vehicles in rigid local frames, and human surfaces through canonical templates. Independent completion leaves the road, neighboring actors, and asset placement to be reconciled after generation.

Our starting point is that a query needs two spatial descriptions. Its canonical coordinate identifies a persistent piece of content; its reference-world coordinate describes where that content lies relative to the surrounding scene. DriveCompose retains both descriptions and lets heterogeneous query groups exchange information during completion (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). Reliable observations become fixed latent anchors, ray evidence identifies free space, and reserved supports represent unobserved content. Type-specific codecs put these supports into a common-width latent space. Local attention follows each support's structure, while global memory communicates scene context between groups.

Joint generation also couples the components through image formation. An unknown surface belongs to one asset, yet it can affect pixels already explained by another. A completed vehicle side can intersect a ray whose background is supported by the log. Observation preservation is therefore a scene-level property, even when the anchors and codec operate query by query. Locking an observed Gaussian's parameters leaves this occlusion route open; a penalty on those parameters cannot measure the effect of added content. We therefore handle parameter preservation and rendered consistency separately. Conditional flow updates only unknown codes, restores observed codes at every step, and clears free-space codes. A fixed per-query decoder transfers these constraints to Gaussian parameters. The decoded candidate is then rendered from the original cameras and compared with both the input images and an observed-only reference. This check measures local image degradation, foreground intrusion, absolute error, and coverage before accepting a completed world.

Acceptance separates construction from use. The world stores a fixed background and one canonical asset per identity. At runtime, the current state places the camera, applies rigid transforms to vehicles, and articulates humans through their fixed skeleton bindings. Geometry, covariance, and viewing directions are transformed consistently. External policy and dynamics update the state; resetting or branching a trajectory reuses the same world and completion seed. This separation makes the completed three-dimensional content, the current actor configuration, and the source of state changes explicit.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A joint completion formulation for background and typed canonical assets, using canonical coordinates for persistent content and reference-world coordinates for scene context.
\item An observation-anchored construction process that combines hard latent constraints and free-space suppression with candidate-versus-reference rendering checks.
\item A fixed Gaussian world representation with consistent state-driven transforms for geometry, covariance, and directional appearance across reused trajectories.
\end{itemize}
