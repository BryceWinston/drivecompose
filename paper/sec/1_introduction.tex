\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Driving simulation and scene editing require a reconstruction that remains usable when the recorded configuration changes. Moving a camera reveals a road patch hidden behind a vehicle. Turning that vehicle exposes its unseen side, and changing a pedestrian's pose brings previously occluded body surfaces into view. These operations require geometry and appearance beyond the recorded images, together with a representation that keeps the content attached to the same scene and actor identities.

Recent feed-forward methods recover driving scenes directly from temporal observations. STORM aggregates dynamic Gaussians through estimated scene flow, and DGGT jointly recovers camera parameters and dynamic scene content from unposed images~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt}. StreetForward and PointForward further study temporal processing and the organization of reconstructed evidence~\cite{yu2026streetforward,chi2026pointforward}. For an asset that will later be moved or articulated, however, observations across time must also be gathered into a stable local representation, and the surfaces exposed by the new state must be supplied.

Compositional representations address the first requirement. Street Gaussians separates vehicles from the background; OmniRe includes canonical models for rigid and articulated actors; HUGSIM connects scene content to control-driven state updates~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire,zhou2024hugsim}. Generative models address missing content through asset priors, Gaussian completion, or generated views~\cite{yang2025genassets,liao2025complete,streetcrafter,zhu2025worldsplat}. Combining them requires a generator that assigns content to the background and to individual assets. A generated surface must have an owner, a coordinate frame, and a motion rule before it can become part of an editable scene.

We formulate driving-scene reconstruction as conditional generation of a compositional scene. Our representation follows the units that will be manipulated: a world-space background, rigid vehicles in local coordinates, and humans on canonical surfaces with skeleton bindings. Image evidence is first aggregated on these supports. A generative model then completes their missing latent content under the observed scene conditions. Each completed surface is decoded in the coordinate frame used to control its component.

DriveCompose implements this formulation with a visual recovery front end, structured latent codes, and a conditional flow model (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). The front end estimates geometry, trajectory-level identities, and time-varying states. Canonical aggregation combines observations of the same content across views and time. Within the completion model, local attention follows each component's support, while shared memory exchanges context between background and assets. Each query carries both its canonical coordinate and its placement in a reference world configuration: the former identifies persistent content, and the latter expresses its spatial relation to the scene. Type-specific decoders convert the completed codes into Gaussian components.

Generation must also respect the images from which the scene was recovered. We retain reliable observed codes and suppress known free space throughout sampling. Newly generated content can nevertheless occlude an observed surface, so we check the assembled candidate in the original views as well. After construction, the background and canonical assets remain fixed. Camera placement, vehicle transforms, and human joint poses determine their subsequent rendering. Learning follows this separation: we fit recovery and the latent representation before training the completion prior, then adapt generation through differentiable rendering while keeping the representation fixed.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A generative reconstruction framework that organizes driving observations into a compositional Gaussian scene, with world-space background and persistent canonical assets for state-driven reuse.
\item A structured completion model that combines category-specific supports and scene-wide context with observed-code constraints, free-space suppression, and rendered observation checks.
\end{itemize}
