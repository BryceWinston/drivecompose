\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Driving simulation and scene editing require 3D scenes that support changes in camera viewpoint, vehicle motion, and human pose~\cite{yang2023unisim,zhou2024hugsim}. However, driving images reveal only the surfaces visible from the recorded viewpoints and actor configurations~\cite{sun2020waymo,caesar2020nuscenes}. Moving a camera, turning a vehicle, or changing a pedestrian's pose exposes previously unobserved regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). Reconstructing scenes for these operations therefore requires completing the missing geometry and appearance while ensuring that completed vehicle and human surfaces move with their respective actors.

Recent feed-forward methods fuse multiple images to directly predict driving-scene geometry and Gaussian representations~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward,chi2026pointforward}. Motion and visibility estimates align dynamic objects and aggregate surfaces visible across the input sequence. Surfaces absent from the entire sequence call for generative priors over geometry and appearance.

Generative reconstruction uses asset priors, Gaussian completion, or generated views to infer unobserved content~\cite{yang2025genassets,liao2025complete,streetcrafter,zhu2025worldsplat}. Compositional reconstruction represents the background and moving actors separately to support vehicle motion and human pose control~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire,zhou2024hugsim}. Completing these component representations lets generated surfaces retain their actors' motion bindings. The visible background and other actors provide scene context for completing a partially observed component.

We introduce DriveCompose, which jointly completes the background, vehicles, and humans as reusable Gaussian components (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). The background is represented in world coordinates, rigid assets in their own frames, and human surfaces in a reference pose with skeletal bindings~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire}. Cross-component memory shares these components' features to condition flow-based completion~\cite{lipman2022flowmatching}. Gaussian decoders preserve their ownership and bindings~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}, allowing completed surfaces to move with their actors under new camera and actor states (Secs.~\ref{sec:representation} and~\ref{sec:composition}).

Experiments on Waymo show higher reconstruction accuracy and better off-trajectory rendering quality than the feed-forward baselines. Paired synthetic tests compare joint completion with scene-level generation and cross-object coupling, while real actor edits demonstrate reuse of the same completed assets (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce DriveCompose to jointly complete backgrounds, vehicles, and humans from image sequences as compositional Gaussian assets, reusable under camera, rigid, and skeletal state changes.
\item We develop a cross-component latent completion model based on conditional flow matching. Local attention models spatial relationships within each component, while cross-component memory aggregates background and actor features to provide scene context for generating each component's missing content.
\item We show that cross-component context benefits completion most when input surface coverage is low, and demonstrate reuse of completed assets across actor-state changes.
\end{itemize}
