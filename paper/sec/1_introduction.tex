\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Driving simulation and scene editing require 3D scenes that support changes in camera viewpoint, vehicle motion, and human pose~\cite{yang2023unisim,zhou2024hugsim}. However, driving images reveal only the surfaces visible from the recorded viewpoints and actor configurations~\cite{sun2020waymo,caesar2020nuscenes}. Moving a camera, turning a vehicle, or changing a pedestrian's pose exposes previously unobserved regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). Reconstructing scenes for these operations therefore requires completing the missing geometry and appearance while ensuring that completed vehicle and human surfaces move with their respective actors.

Recent feed-forward methods fuse multiple images to directly predict driving-scene geometry and Gaussian representations~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward,chi2026pointforward}. Motion and visibility estimates align dynamic objects and aggregate surfaces visible across the input sequence. When a new viewpoint or actor motion reveals surfaces unseen throughout the sequence, reconstruction must infer their geometry and appearance.

Generative reconstruction uses asset priors, Gaussian completion, or generated views to infer unobserved content~\cite{yang2025genassets,liao2025complete,streetcrafter,zhu2025worldsplat}. Meanwhile, compositional reconstruction represents the background and moving actors separately to support vehicle motion and human pose control~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire,zhou2024hugsim}. Applying generative completion to such scenes requires generated surfaces to move with their actors. When an actor is only partially observed, information from the background and other actors can provide additional context for inferring its missing content.

We introduce DriveCompose, which reconstructs driving image sequences into a generatively completed 3D scene with independently controllable actors. We represent the background in world coordinates, rigid surfaces in each asset's own coordinate frame, and human surfaces in a reference pose with skeletal bindings~\cite{yan2024street,chen2025omnire}. Completion operates directly on these representations, allowing generated rigid and human surfaces to follow their actors' motion and pose changes (Sec.~\ref{sec:representation}). The model jointly completes missing background and actor content using shared scene context.

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes construction and reuse. A visual encoder recovers geometry and motion; canonical aggregation aligns observations, and type-specific encoders produce component latents. Local attention models each component, while cross-component memory exchanges scene context to complete missing content through flow matching~\cite{lipman2022flowmatching}. Gaussian decoders retain rigid and skeletal bindings~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}, allowing state changes to reuse the same completed content (Sec.~\ref{sec:composition}).

Under shared camera and actor states on Waymo, DriveCompose improves reconstruction and off-trajectory rendering. Paired synthetic tests show better newly visible surfaces than independent completion; real edits reuse the same completed assets across actor states (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce DriveCompose to jointly complete backgrounds, vehicles, and humans from image sequences as compositional Gaussian assets, reusable under camera, rigid, and skeletal state changes.
\item We develop a cross-component latent completion model based on conditional flow matching. Local attention models spatial relationships within each component, while cross-component memory aggregates background and actor features to provide scene context for generating each component's missing content.
\item We evaluate conditional reconstruction, off-trajectory rendering, asset edits, and component ablations. Visibility-stratified diagnostics localize the gains from joint completion to severely missing background and actor regions.
\end{itemize}
