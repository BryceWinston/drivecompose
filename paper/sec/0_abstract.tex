\begin{abstract}
Driving logs observe only part of a scene, whereas simulation exposes surfaces through new camera positions and actor states. Completing these surfaces requires coordinating world-space background with canonical vehicles and articulated humans while retaining the recorded appearance. We present DriveCompose, which builds a composable Gaussian world by jointly completing typed canonical queries. A shared recovery front end supplies geometry, identity, and state evidence. Local query groups preserve each category's spatial structure, and global memory exchanges context across background and assets. Conditional flow fills unknown latent codes, restores observed codes after every step, and suppresses known free space. A fixed per-query decoder preserves the observed Gaussian parameters; a separate rendering check detects degradation caused by newly added content. Once accepted, the background and canonical assets remain fixed. Camera poses, rigid transforms, and skeletal poses then drive rendering across trajectories that share the same completion realization. This formulation places generation in offline world construction and makes subsequent scene changes explicit through state-driven composition.
\end{abstract}
