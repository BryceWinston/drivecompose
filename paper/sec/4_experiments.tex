\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Waymo experiments test reconstruction accuracy and off-trajectory rendering quality. Paired hidden-surface tests and actor edits examine joint completion and reuse of fixed assets. The supplementary material reports zero-shot transfer to nuScenes.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
\paragraph{Data and supervision.}
We divide Waymo's 798 training sequences into 740 training and 58 development sequences, evaluating on 202 validation sequences. Synthetic training uses 10,000 scenes, 80 maps, 10,000 vehicles, and 2,500 human identities. Source maps, vehicle models, and human identities are split before creating appearance/motion variants; real logs are separated by road segment, capture, and identifiable actors.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
STORM, DGGT, and StreetForward~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward} are retrained from random initialization on the same real logs, synthetic scenes, and complete assets. Each uses its native supervision; Ours also uses cell-empty and template-binding targets. A shared state adapter assigns ownership and canonicalizes predicted Gaussians for rigid and skeletal placement. Methods share context RGB, calibration, ego poses, vehicle boxes/root tracks, and target human joints; shape and binding come from context. Test-time weights are fixed. DGGT denotes its Gaussian branch (DGGT-G).

\paragraph{Sampling and metrics.}
Each test sequence supplies 200 frames, forming 2,020 disjoint 20-frame clips at 10 Hz. Three front cameras provide context at $\{0,5,10,15\}$~\cite{yang2025storm}; the remaining 12 interpolation and four extrapolation frames give 96,960 targets. Extrapolation renders supplied states. We evaluate $240\times160$ images~\cite{huang2026instantnurec} using full/dynamic PSNR and SSIM, and depth AbsRel/$\delta_1$ at valid LiDAR points. Depth is aligned per frame for evaluation. Frames, clips, then sequences are averaged with equal sequence weight.

\subsection{Off-trajectory rendering}
\label{sec:spatial_evaluation}
We shift cameras laterally by $\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm4$ m while fixing orientation, intrinsics, and timestamps~\cite{chi2026pointforward,zhu2025worldsplat}. We compare Gaussian branches and full generative pipelines. Each direction contains 121,200 images and 6,060 20-frame videos at 10 Hz, referenced to recordings from the same clips, cameras, and timestamps. Left/right FID and FVD are computed separately, then averaged (Table~\ref{tab:spatial_current}).

Full generative controls include DGGT-Full and STORM+Difix~\cite{wu2025difix3d}. DriveCompose achieves lower FID and FVD than all compared methods at each of the three camera offsets (Table~\ref{tab:spatial_current}; Fig.~\ref{fig:waymo-left-shift}). Fixed-reference evaluation further tests scene correspondence, where DriveCompose obtains the lowest RGB MAE and LPIPS (Appendix~H).

\subsection{State-driven asset reuse}
\label{sec:ablation-reuse}
\paragraph{Asset edits.}
The same completed assets are reused for a 1 m camera shift, a $30^\circ$ vehicle rotation, and a human pose change. On twenty real scenes, each containing one vehicle and one human, 82.78\% of 360 actor edits jointly pass structure, new-surface texture, identity, and geometry, compared with 80.28\% for Asset Harvester~\cite{cao2026assetharvester}. Structural integrity passes 92.78\% of actor edits; Asset Harvester leads identity preservation by 0.83 percentage points. Across six edit levels, joint acceptance is 74.54\%, 4.91 points above Asset Harvester. Camera shifts are evaluated separately; Appendix~I gives the seed protocol and detailed counts.

\paragraph{Observation preservation.}
\label{sec:observation-preservation}
Compared with the observed-only scene at fixed cameras and actor states, Ours preserves observed Gaussian parameters exactly, while input-view PSNR decreases by 0.03 dB and depth MAE increases by 2 mm. Previously observed human texture has canonical linear-RGB MAE 0.014.

\paragraph{Efficiency.}
On an RTX 4090 (FP16, batch 1, rule C), construction takes $2.66\pm0.11$ s, single-camera composition/rendering $7.8\pm0.4$ ms at $240\times160$, and peak memory 14.2 GB. Reusing the constructed assets amortizes the total cost over 1,000 outputs to 10.46 ms each. At $480\times320$ input and output, Ours exceeds every external method by at least 1.49 dB full-image PSNR and renders one image in 13.71 ms, versus 161.87 ms for DGGT-Full (Appendix~K).

\subsection{Ablation studies}
\label{sec:ablation-protocol}\label{sec:ablations}\label{sec:ablation-studies}
Waymo ablations use one 29-timestamp window per test sequence, three cameras, and seven shared target timestamps.

\paragraph{Completion components.}
The w/o GCM control disables non-observed, non-sky Gaussians. The w/o CCM control uses isolated per-component memories with the same parameters and global log context, retraining Stages~\mbox{3--4}. Variants share recovery, codec, states, data, and optimization budgets; Appendix~G specifies sampling and seed aggregation. At $T=8$, exchanging component context adds 0.627 and 0.934 dB in full-image and dynamic-region PSNR, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:ablation-components}; Fig.~\ref{fig:component-ablation}).

\paragraph{Cross-component context.}
The benefit of shared context increases as input surface coverage decreases. Compared with w/o CCM, the PSNR gain rises from 0.03--0.05 dB on well-observed regions to 1.02--1.53 dB on severely missing regions in 100 synthetic scenes. On paired hidden-region tests, shared context exceeds task-adapted object-coupling and scene-latent completion by 0.67 and 0.94 dB, respectively. With matched queries, dense attention is 0.05 dB higher, while CCM completes $1.93\times$ faster with 42.71\% lower peak memory (Appendix~H).
