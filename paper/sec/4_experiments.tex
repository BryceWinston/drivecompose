\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We evaluate conditional reconstruction on Waymo, off-trajectory rendering, and reuse of completed assets. Component, visibility, and observation-preservation tests examine where joint completion helps. Appendix A reports zero-shot transfer to nuScenes.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
\paragraph{Data and supervision.}
We divide Waymo's 798 training sequences into 740 training and 58 development sequences, evaluating on 202 validation sequences. Synthetic training uses 10,000 scenes, 80 maps, 10,000 vehicles, and 2,500 human identities. Source maps, vehicle models, and human identities are split before creating appearance/motion variants; real logs are separated by road segment, capture, and identifiable actors. Appendix B gives all split sizes and supervision.

\paragraph{Controlled comparison.}
STORM, DGGT, and StreetForward~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward} are retrained from random initialization on the same real logs, synthetic scenes, and complete assets. Each uses its native supervision; Ours also uses cell-empty and template-binding targets. A shared state adapter assigns ownership and canonicalizes predicted Gaussians for rigid and skeletal placement. Methods share context RGB, calibration, ego poses, vehicle boxes/root tracks, and target human joints; shape and binding come from context. Test-time weights are fixed. Appendix F reports training costs and adaptation details.

\paragraph{Sampling and metrics.}
Each test sequence supplies 200 frames, forming 2,020 disjoint 20-frame clips at 10 Hz. Three front cameras provide context at $\{0,5,10,15\}$~\cite{yang2025storm}; the remaining 12 interpolation and four extrapolation frames give 96,960 targets. Extrapolation renders supplied states. We evaluate $240\times160$ images~\cite{huang2026instantnurec} using full/dynamic PSNR and SSIM, and depth AbsRel/$\delta_1$ at valid LiDAR points. Depth is aligned per frame for evaluation; Appendix F also reports unaligned results. Frames, clips, then sequences are averaged with equal sequence weight.

\subsection{Off-trajectory rendering and missing surfaces}
\label{sec:spatial_evaluation}
We shift cameras laterally by $\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm4$ m while fixing orientation, intrinsics, and timestamps~\cite{chi2026pointforward,zhu2025worldsplat}. Gaussian renders are compared before diffusion refinement. Each direction contains 121,200 images and 6,060 20-frame videos at 10 Hz, referenced to recordings from the same clips, cameras, and timestamps. Left/right FID and FVD are computed separately, then averaged (Table~\ref{tab:spatial_current}; Appendix H).

Figure~\ref{fig:waymo-left-shift} compares shifted renders against an original-trajectory reference. For paired evaluation, 100 synthetic test scenes provide regions unseen in every input. At $\pm2$ m, Ours improves hidden-region PSNR from 21.56 to 23.18 dB over w/o CCM, reduces LPIPS from 0.236 to 0.192, and raises surface coverage from 89.7\% to 95.0\% and F-score@2 cm from 81.3\% to 88.6\%. On real scenes, Ours reduces common-support RGB reprojection MAE from 0.028 to 0.019 versus w/o CCM and raises coverage from 86.9\% to 92.4\%. Appendix H defines surface extraction and correspondence evaluation.

\subsection{Ablations and component context}
\label{sec:ablation-protocol}\label{sec:ablations}\label{sec:ablation-studies}
\paragraph{Protocol.}
Ablations use one 29-timestamp window per test sequence, three cameras, nested inputs of $T=2,4,8$ timestamps, and seven shared targets (Appendix G). Variants share recovery, codec, states, data, and optimization budgets. Training samples $T$ uniformly; inference uses 24 Euler steps. Each of three training seeds is evaluated with three generation seeds, all averaged without best-sample selection. All 10,908 learned and 202 deterministic window instances are renderable; poor-quality outputs remain scored.

\paragraph{Completion and context.}
The w/o GCM control disables non-observed, non-sky Gaussians. The w/o CCM control replaces shared memory with isolated per-component memories, retaining the same parameters and global log context, and is retrained through Stages 3--4. Table~\ref{tab:ablation-components} shows full-image/dynamic PSNR gains of 1.776/2.186 dB over w/o GCM and 0.627/0.934 dB over w/o CCM. Full-image PSNR training-seed SDs are 0.061 and 0.074 dB for Ours and w/o CCM. At $T=2,4,8$, fixed-checkpoint PSNR gains are 1.104, 0.738, and 0.627 dB; the corresponding LPIPS reductions are 0.041, 0.028, and 0.018 (Appendix G).

\paragraph{Where CCM helps.}
On the 100 synthetic scenes, input-union surface coverage defines well-observed ($\geq80\%$) and severely missing ($\leq20\%$) groups. CCM adds 1.02/1.28/1.54 dB for severely missing background/vehicle/human regions, versus 0.04/0.04/0.03 dB for well-observed regions. On Waymo, inference-time read masks on the same Ours checkpoints yield 28.721/28.574/28.352 dB for background/other vehicles/other humans, versus 28.913 with all components; the retrained w/o CCM scores 28.286. Appendix H details the read-scope interventions.

\subsection{State-driven reuse and observation preservation}
\label{sec:ablation-reuse}
\paragraph{Asset edits.}
Twenty real and twenty identity-disjoint synthetic scenes each contain one tested vehicle and human. A 1 m camera shift, $30^\circ$ vehicle rotation, and human pose change reuse fixed completed assets. Table~\ref{tab:asset-reuse} evaluates newly visible synthetic surfaces at 2 cm vehicle and 1 cm human coverage thresholds.

Across nine training/generation-seed combinations, real edits succeed for 177/180 vehicles and 157/180 humans, yielding 92.78\% overall success. A scene-cluster bootstrap gives a 95\% interval of 85.28--99.17\%. Vehicle dimensions and human shape remain fixed; previously observed human texture has canonical linear-RGB MAE 0.014. Appendix I reports individual seeds, blinded failure ratings, and elbow, self-occlusion, and clothing failures.

\paragraph{Observation preservation.}
\label{sec:observation-preservation}
Compared with the observed-only scene at fixed cameras and actor states, Ours preserves observed Gaussian parameters exactly, while input-view PSNR decreases by 0.029 dB and depth MAE increases by 2 mm. On 2,020,000 fixed rays, added-occlusion and strict free-ray violation rates are 0.18\% and 0.07\%; removing the free-ray loss raises them to 1.34\% and 0.91\%. Appendix J reports parameter and rendering measurements.

\paragraph{Efficiency.}
On an RTX 4090 (FP16, batch 1), construction takes $2.66\pm0.11$ s and single-camera state composition/rendering takes $7.8\pm0.4$ ms at $240\times160$, with 14.2 GB peak memory. CCM adds 5.1\% construction time. The 1,000-query amortized cost is 10.46 ms per image. Appendix K specifies scene size, synchronization, warmups, and repetitions.
