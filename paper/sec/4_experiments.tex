\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Waymo experiments test reconstruction accuracy and off-trajectory rendering quality. Paired hidden-surface tests and actor edits examine joint completion and reuse of fixed assets. Main results use center-ray rule C; footprint rule F is a separate controlled comparison. Appendix A reports zero-shot transfer to nuScenes.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
\paragraph{Data and supervision.}
We divide Waymo's 798 training sequences into 740 training and 58 development sequences, evaluating on 202 validation sequences. Synthetic training uses 10,000 scenes, 80 maps, 10,000 vehicles, and 2,500 human identities. Source maps, vehicle models, and human identities are split before creating appearance/motion variants; real logs are separated by road segment, capture, and identifiable actors. Appendix B gives all split sizes and supervision.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
STORM, DGGT, and StreetForward~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward} are retrained from random initialization on the same real logs, synthetic scenes, and complete assets. Each uses its native supervision; Ours also uses cell-empty and template-binding targets. A shared state adapter assigns ownership and canonicalizes predicted Gaussians for rigid and skeletal placement. Methods share context RGB, calibration, ego poses, vehicle boxes/root tracks, and target human joints; shape and binding come from context. Test-time weights are fixed. Appendix F reports training costs and adaptation details.

\paragraph{Sampling and metrics.}
Each test sequence supplies 200 frames, forming 2,020 disjoint 20-frame clips at 10 Hz. Three front cameras provide context at $\{0,5,10,15\}$~\cite{yang2025storm}; the remaining 12 interpolation and four extrapolation frames give 96,960 targets. Extrapolation renders supplied states. We evaluate $240\times160$ images~\cite{huang2026instantnurec} using full/dynamic PSNR and SSIM, and depth AbsRel/$\delta_1$ at valid LiDAR points. Depth is aligned per frame for evaluation; Appendix F also reports unaligned results. Frames, clips, then sequences are averaged with equal sequence weight.

\subsection{Off-trajectory rendering}
\label{sec:spatial_evaluation}
We shift cameras laterally by $\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm4$ m while fixing orientation, intrinsics, and timestamps~\cite{chi2026pointforward,zhu2025worldsplat}. Gaussian renders are compared before diffusion refinement. Each direction contains 121,200 images and 6,060 20-frame videos at 10 Hz, referenced to recordings from the same clips, cameras, and timestamps. Left/right FID and FVD are computed separately, then averaged (Table~\ref{tab:spatial_current}; Appendix H).

DriveCompose achieves the lowest FID and FVD at all three Waymo offsets, improving both image and video distribution quality (Table~\ref{tab:spatial_current}). Figure~\ref{fig:waymo-left-shift} compares shifted renders against an original-trajectory reference.

\subsection{State-driven asset reuse}
\label{sec:ablation-reuse}
\paragraph{Asset edits.}
We test fixed-asset reuse on twenty real scenes, each containing one vehicle and one human. A 1 m camera shift, $30^\circ$ vehicle rotation, and human pose change reuse fixed completed assets. Nine training/generation-seed combinations yield 360 actor edits. The five-pixel structural-integrity criterion passes 334/360 (92.78\%); 298/360 (82.78\%) jointly pass structure, newly visible texture, appearance identity, and local geometry checks. Appendix I reports the four-of-five rating rule, failure breakdown, and seed statistics.

\paragraph{Observation preservation.}
Compared with the observed-only scene at fixed cameras and actor states, Ours preserves observed Gaussian parameters exactly, while input-view PSNR decreases by 0.029 dB and depth MAE increases by 2 mm. Previously observed human texture has canonical linear-RGB MAE 0.014. Appendix J reports parameter and rendering measurements.

\paragraph{Efficiency.}
On an RTX 4090 (FP16, batch 1, rule C), construction takes $2.66\pm0.11$ s, single-camera composition/rendering $7.8\pm0.4$ ms at $240\times160$, and peak memory 14.2 GB. Reusing the constructed assets amortizes the total cost over 1,000 outputs to 10.46 ms each. Appendix K specifies timing conditions.

\subsection{Ablation studies}
\label{sec:ablation-protocol}\label{sec:ablations}\label{sec:ablation-studies}
The w/o GCM control disables non-observed, non-sky Gaussians. The w/o CCM control uses isolated per-component memories with the same parameters and global log context, retraining Stages~\mbox{3--4}.

\paragraph{Completion components.}
Waymo ablations use one 29-timestamp window per test sequence, three cameras, nested $T=2,4,8$ inputs, and seven shared targets (Appendix G). Variants share recovery, codec, states, data, and optimization budgets. Training samples $T$ uniformly; inference uses 24 Euler steps. We average three training and three generation seeds, retaining every output in the scores. Exchanging component context adds 0.627/0.934 dB full-image/dynamic PSNR (Table~\ref{tab:ablation-components}); training-seed SDs are 0.061 and 0.074 dB for Ours and w/o CCM. PSNR gains are 1.104, 0.738, and 0.627 dB at $T=2,4,8$, with the largest benefit at the sparsest input setting (Appendix G). CCM adds 5.1\% construction time (Appendix K).

\paragraph{Hidden-surface completion.}
\label{sec:hidden-completion}
One hundred synthetic test scenes provide paired hidden-region evaluation at $\pm2$ m. We adapt scene-level latent generation~\cite{liao2025complete} and cross-object latent coupling~\cite{wu2026jrm} to shared recovery, codec, query layout, inputs, supervision, and sampling. Three training and three generation seeds are averaged. Joint completion improves both appearance and surface recovery, with gains of 0.67 dB PSNR, 2.6 points coverage, and 3.6 points F-score over object coupling (Table~\ref{tab:completion-mechanisms}). On real common support, RGB reprojection MAE falls from 0.028 to 0.019 versus w/o CCM while coverage rises from 86.9\% to 92.4\%. Appendix H gives the mechanism interfaces and geometry evaluation.

\paragraph{Visibility-stratified analysis.}
On the 100 synthetic scenes, input-union surface coverage defines well-observed ($\geq80\%$) and severely missing ($\leq20\%$) groups. CCM adds 1.02/1.28/1.54 dB for severely missing background/vehicle/human regions, versus 0.04/0.04/0.03 dB for well-observed regions. Appendix H reports fixed-checkpoint read-scope interventions.

\paragraph{Free-space constraints.}
\label{sec:observation-preservation}
On 2,020,000 fixed rays, added-occlusion and strict free-ray violation rates are 0.18\% and 0.07\%; removing the free-ray loss raises them to 1.34\% and 0.91\% (Appendix J). With weights, layout, depth, and per-query noise fixed, F reduces occupied-cell mislabels from 1,374 to 19. Hidden-region coverage rises from 95.0\% to 96.2\%, PSNR from 23.18 to 23.36 dB, and construction time from 2.66 to 2.86 s. Appendix D separates cell errors, surface coverage, and ray violations.
