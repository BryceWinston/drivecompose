\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We study dynamic scene reconstruction from sparse temporal observations along two axes: recovering scene appearance at unobserved times and rendering the reconstructed scene from off-trajectory viewpoints. Temporal evaluation separates interpolation from short-horizon prediction, while spatial evaluation tests rendering quality as the camera moves away from the recorded trajectory.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\paragraph{Dataset and temporal sampling.}
We evaluate on a fixed subset of 40 sequences from the Waymo Open Dataset scene-flow validation split. We adopt the 20-frame temporal sampling pattern used in STORM and the evaluation resolution used in Instant NuRec~\cite{yang2025storm,huang2026instantnurec}. Each clip contains 20 consecutive frames at 10\,Hz. We use the FRONT camera and provide four context images at zero-based frame indices $\{0,5,10,15\}$. Temporal interpolation is evaluated at indices $\{1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,11,12,13,14\}$, and temporal extrapolation at $\{16,17,18,19\}$. The latter corresponds to prediction horizons of 0.1--0.4 seconds beyond the last observation. Context frames are excluded from temporal reconstruction scores.

\paragraph{Baselines and inference settings.}
The current comparison includes STORM, DGGT, and StreetForward using their released checkpoints without additional fine-tuning~\cite{yang2025storm,chen2025dggt,yu2026streetforward}. All methods observe the same camera and context timestamps and render the same target views. STORM uses calibrated cameras during reconstruction. DGGT and StreetForward estimate cameras and use context-based alignment and intrinsic-calibration adapters before rendering. DGGT is evaluated without diffusion refinement; its temporal adapter extends the last observed tracked motion for extrapolation. These settings define the implementations evaluated here.

\paragraph{Image and depth metrics.}
RGB predictions and reference images are evaluated at $240\times160$ pixels. We report PSNR and SSIM over the full image and within shared 2D annotation-box masks. These masks define annotated regions, rather than motion-segmented foreground. For depth, we report AbsRel and $\delta_1$ on valid projected LiDAR samples after per-frame least-squares scale alignment, following the metric formulation in Instant NuRec~\cite{huang2026instantnurec}. Frame scores are averaged within each window, window scores within each sequence, and sequence scores with equal weight across the 40 sequences.

\paragraph{Preprocessing and calibration.}
The current STORM pipeline resizes quarter-resolution JPEG images to $240\times160$; DGGT and StreetForward resize the original JPEG images to $518\times350$ and $504\times336$, respectively. Each method retains its input normalization. Camera alignment uses the four context observations. In temporal evaluation, one DGGT window uses context-frame LiDAR to resolve an otherwise indeterminate metric scale. In spatial evaluation, DGGT and StreetForward additionally use context-frame LiDAR scale calibration when camera motion is too small or trajectory alignment is unreliable. Calibration excludes the shared annotated regions and uses no target-frame depth or RGB.

\subsection{Temporal Reconstruction and Prediction}
\label{sec:temporal_evaluation}

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
We traverse non-overlapping 20-frame windows throughout each selected sequence. Incomplete or temporally invalid windows are excluded using the shared data checks, yielding 357 valid windows for each method. We report interpolation, extrapolation, and all-target scores separately. The all-target result combines the 12 interpolated frames and four extrapolated frames in each window.

\paragraph{Quantitative comparison.}
Table~\ref{tab:temporal_current} reports the measured baseline results. STORM and DGGT achieve all-target PSNR values of 26.191 and 26.056\,dB, respectively, while StreetForward achieves 25.256\,dB. All three methods obtain lower PSNR on extrapolation than on interpolation. Table~\ref{tab:regions_current} complements the full-image comparison with annotated-region scores and scale-aligned depth metrics.

\subsection{Off-Trajectory View Synthesis}
\label{sec:spatial_evaluation}

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
We evaluate spatial extrapolation on the first 20-frame window of each of the same 40 sequences. Starting from the recorded camera trajectory, we apply fixed lateral offsets of $\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm4$ meters in the local camera frame while retaining camera orientation, intrinsics, and timestamps. Together with the original trajectory, this produces seven 20-frame videos per scene and 280 videos per method. This experiment follows the lateral-shift evaluation strategy of WorldSplat and the extrapolated-view evaluation motivation of PointForward~\cite{zhu2025worldsplat,chi2026pointforward}.

\paragraph{Distributional evaluation.}
The shifted trajectories do not have paired reference images. We therefore compute FID and FVD against a fixed population of images and videos from the original recorded trajectories. FID uses the four context timestamps, yielding 160 images per offset and method, to assess spatial changes at observed times. FVD uses the consecutive frames 0--15 at 10\,Hz, yielding 40 clips per offset and method and incorporating temporal interpolation. We use Inception image features for FID and I3D video features for FVD. Scores are computed over the pooled sample population for each offset; left and right shifts are reported separately. All methods share the same scenes and real reference population. Lower scores indicate smaller feature-distribution differences; paired visualizations assess scene-specific structure and viewpoint response.

\paragraph{Quantitative comparison.}
Table~\ref{tab:spatial_current} reports both metrics. STORM obtains the lowest FID and FVD at the one- and two-meter offsets, whereas StreetForward obtains the lowest values at both four-meter offsets. The ordering therefore depends on the displacement. The original-trajectory scores provide a reference for changes under viewpoint shifts; the spatial results use their own calibrated rendering pipeline and sample scope, rather than the all-window temporal evaluation.
