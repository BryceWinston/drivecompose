\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

DriveCompose aggregates observations in world and canonical coordinates, jointly completes their latents, and decodes reusable Gaussian assets for rendering under new states (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).



\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:representation}

The input is a synchronized image log $\mathbf I_{t,v}$ with timestamps, camera intrinsics, and camera-to-rig calibration. The controlled evaluations provide rig poses, instance identities, vehicle boxes and root trajectories, and target human joints. Human shape and binding are recovered from context; no target RGB or test LiDAR enters reconstruction. Coordinate conventions and image preprocessing are detailed in Appendix D of the supplementary material.

The output is a compositional Gaussian scene

\begin{equation}
\mathcal W=\bigl(\mathcal G_b,C_{\rm sky},V_{\rm sky},
\{\mathcal A_a\},\mathcal B,\xi\bigr),
\label{eq:world}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal G_b$ is the world-space background, $C_{\rm sky}$ a sky cubemap, $V_{\rm sky}$ its supported directions, $\mathcal A_a$ the canonical asset for identity $a$, $\mathcal B$ the finite spatial support, and $\xi$ the sampling seed. The components are rendered by Gaussian splatting~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}. Vehicles are represented as rigid assets with fixed local geometry and dimensions. Humans additionally retain a rest skeleton and skinning weights. Scene state controls the placement and articulation of these fixed components.

We model missing content through conditional generation. Let $\mathcal Q$ be the recovered query layout and $C$ its observed evidence. The completion model samples unknown codes $\mathbf z_u$, while the codec $D_\psi$ decodes the assembled codes into scene content:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf z_u\sim p_\theta(\mathbf z_u\mid C,\mathcal Q),
\qquad
\mathcal W=D_\psi(\mathbf z^{\rm obs},\mathbf z_u;\mathcal Q).
\label{eq:generative-reconstruction}
\end{equation}

The query layout assigns each latent code to a spatial location and scene component. Completion fills the unknown codes within this layout, and decoding preserves their component identities and motion bindings.

\subsection{Visual recovery and canonical aggregation}
\label{sec:visual-encoding}

The Driving Log Encoder extracts patch features from each image and exchanges pooled context across cameras and timestamps~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit,vaswani2017attention}. Geometry heads predict camera-axis depth, normals, confidence, semantics, and instance assignments, linking image features to 3D surfaces and their owning components. Trajectory-level identity slots separate persistent identity, dimensions, and human shape from frame-dependent root poses and joint rotations. Architectural details and state parameterization are given in Appendix D of the supplementary material.

\label{sec:queries}

\paragraph{Spatial supports.}

A query stores canonical position $\mathbf q_i$, cell scale $\boldsymbol\delta_i$, type $k_i$, local attention group $g_i$, and reference-world coordinate $\mathbf q_i^w$. Reliable depth samples provide surface anchors. Background anchors are combined with a finite world grid; rigid anchors are aligned by inverse SE(3) transforms and combined with a grid in the local bounding volume. Human queries lie on the canonical template surface. Sky queries are unit directions grouped by cubemap face. These supports provide explicit locations for both observed and missing content.

The layout retains canonical coordinates to identify component surfaces and first-input world coordinates $\mathbf q_i^w$, including human skinning, to locate them relative to the background and other actors.

\paragraph{Motion-aware evidence aggregation.}

To aggregate evidence for the same surface across time, we place each canonical query at the corresponding actor state:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf p_i(t)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbf q_i,& k_i=\mathrm{background},\\
\operatorname{xyz}(\mathbf T^w_a(t)\bar{\mathbf q}_i),& k_i=\mathrm{rigid},\\
\operatorname{xyz}(\mathbf T^w_h(t)\mathbf A_i(t)\bar{\mathbf q}_i),& k_i=\mathrm{human}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:query-placement}
\end{equation}

Here $\bar{\mathbf q}_i$ is homogeneous, $\operatorname{xyz}$ extracts Euclidean coordinates, and $\mathbf A_i(t)$ is the weighted skinning transform. Projection gives query depth $z_{i,t,v}$ and samples image features $\boldsymbol\phi_{i,t,v}$, depth $d_{i,t,v}$, and confidence $c_{i,t,v}$. We weight observations by confidence and normalized depth residual $r=(d-z)/(\eta_{\rm abs}+\eta_{\rm rel}d)$. The indicator $a_{i,t,v}$ selects visible, depth-consistent observations of the same instance (Appendix D):

\begin{align}
w_{i,t,v}&=a_{i,t,v}c_{i,t,v}\exp(-r_{i,t,v}^2/2),\\
\mathbf h_i&=\frac{\sum_{t,v}w_{i,t,v}[\boldsymbol\phi_{i,t,v};r_{i,t,v}]}
{\max(\varepsilon,\sum_{t,v}w_{i,t,v})}.
\label{eq:aggregation}
\end{align}

The masks $m_i$, $f_i$, and $u_i$ distinguish observed surfaces, ray-labeled free space, and unknown content, with $m_i+f_i+u_i=1$. Confidence is the maximum over valid observations; two reliable depth-ray votes establish a free-space label. Our main protocol uses center-ray voting (C); the footprint alternative (F) tests the projected cell extent. Sky features are aggregated by viewing direction using camera rotation and sky semantics. Appendix D reports both rules and their downstream effects.

\subsection{Joint latent completion}
\label{sec:codec}
\label{sec:completion}

\paragraph{Latent encoding.}

Type-specific encoders map the 129-dimensional aggregate and its spatial support, $[\mathbf h_i;\mathbf q_i;\boldsymbol\delta_i]$, through a $135\!\rightarrow\!128\!\rightarrow\!128$ MLP with LayerNorm. All types use $D=128$ codes, allowing joint completion while retaining query type, ownership, and frame. Partial and complete branches share this map; masks and confidence condition completion separately. Unobserved codes are zeroed, and observed codes are denoted $\mathbf z^{\rm obs}$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fig03-joint-completion-core-boxed.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Joint latent completion.} CCM reads local features and writes back shared context; projected flow updates only unknown codes.}
  \label{fig:joint-completion}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Cross-component memory.}

Our generative completion module (GCM) combines a scene-conditioned flow transformer with constrained sampling (Fig.~\ref{fig:joint-completion}). We parameterize its completion velocity with a DiT-style transformer~\cite{peebles2023dit} trained by flow matching~\cite{lipman2022flowmatching}. Its input combines current and observed codes, masks, confidence, type, both coordinate descriptions, cell scales, and pooled log features. Background groups follow spatial neighborhoods, each asset forms a local group, and sky directions are grouped by cubemap face. Local self-attention processes these groups separately. Cross-component memory (CCM) then exchanges context across groups:

\begin{align}
\mathbf M'&=\mathbf M+\operatorname{Attn}_{r}
(\mathbf M,\widetilde{\mathbf X},\widetilde{\mathbf X}),\\
\mathbf U&=\widetilde{\mathbf X}+\operatorname{Attn}_{w}
(\operatorname{LN}(\widetilde{\mathbf X}),\mathbf M',\mathbf M').
\label{eq:memory-exchange}
\end{align}

Here $\widetilde{\mathbf X}$ concatenates local tokens; attention arguments are queries, keys, and values. Memory first reads all local groups, then supplies the updated scene context to each group's tokens. A residual feed-forward update follows $\mathbf U$. Flow time modulates local normalization, and learned tokens plus log summaries initialize memory.

\paragraph{Flow training and constrained sampling.}

Let $\mathbf z_1$ be a complete target on the same query layout and $\boldsymbol\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbf I)$ be Gaussian noise. We interpolate only the unknown content at flow time $\tau\in[0,1]$:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf z_\tau=m\odot\mathbf z^{\rm obs}
+u\odot[(1-\tau)\boldsymbol\epsilon+\tau\mathbf z_1].
\label{eq:flow-path}
\end{equation}

Observed codes remain fixed and free-space codes remain zero. The target velocity for unknown queries is $\mathbf z_1-\boldsymbol\epsilon$. With an optional loss selector $s_i$, the objective is

\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\rm flow}=
\frac{\sum_i u_i s_i\|\mathbf v_\theta(\mathbf z_\tau,\tau,C)_i
-(\mathbf z_{1,i}-\boldsymbol\epsilon_i)\|_2^2}
{D\max(1,\sum_i u_i s_i)}.
\label{eq:flow-loss}
\end{equation}

Prior learning uses samples with valid targets at every unknown query; $s_i$ selects loss terms within these targets (Sec.~\ref{sec:training}).

At inference, we initialize unknown codes with noise and take $S$ Euler steps with $\Delta\tau=1/S$:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf z^{j+1}=\Pi_{m,f}\!\left[
\mathbf z^j+\Delta\tau\,u\odot
\mathbf v_\theta(\mathbf z^j,j/S,C)\right].
\label{eq:sampling}
\end{equation}

After every step, $\Pi_{m,f}$ restores observed codes and clears free-space codes. The seed fixes a completion, decoded once for reuse across states.

\paragraph{Type-specific Gaussian decoding.}
\label{sec:decoding}

Background, rigid, and human Gaussian decoders independently map each query to $K=4$ child Gaussians whose centers lie in its support cell. Human children inherit the query's skinning weights; rigid children share their asset's rigid transform. Opacity is multiplied by $1-f_i$, so free queries cannot produce visible Gaussians. A separate sky head predicts cubemap colors within the observation-supported domain $V_{\rm sky}$. Gaussian and appearance parameterizations are detailed in Appendix E of the supplementary material.

Because decoding is query-wise, freezing the codec and retaining observed codes preserves observed Gaussian parameters. Free-ray losses constrain the visibility and alpha-compositing effects of added Gaussians, measured in Sec.~\ref{sec:observation-preservation}.

\subsection{State-driven scene composition}
\label{sec:composition}

At time step $k$, state $s_k$ specifies the ego pose, active assets, rigid transforms, and human root and joint poses. Rigid transforms update canonical Gaussian centers and covariances. Human joint poses deform them through the stored skinning weights, so completed surfaces move with the corresponding body parts. Appendix C gives the transformations. The renderer composites the background, active assets, and valid sky:

\begin{equation}
o_k=\mathcal R(\operatorname{Compose}(\mathcal W,s_k),\mathcal C_k).
\label{eq:simulation}
\end{equation}

The background and canonical assets remain fixed; new camera and actor states reuse the same completed content.

\subsection{Training procedure}
\label{sec:training}
We train recovery and the codec before learning the completion prior and adapting the GCM through rendering. Complete targets use the partial input's query layout $\mathcal Q$, including its cell scales, component ownership, and human bindings. Complete meshes supply surface samples within each cell. Multiview rendering and the frozen visual encoder produce $\mathbf h_i^{\rm full}$, which the shared type-specific encoder maps to $\mathbf z_{1,i}=E_{k_i}([\mathbf h_i^{\rm full};\mathbf q_i;\boldsymbol\delta_i])$. Surface-free cells receive zero features and opacity supervision.

After codec fitting, recovery and the codec remain frozen. Stage 3 learns from complete synthetic targets at every unknown query. Stage 4 updates only the GCM, mixing complete synthetic samples and the 740 real training logs at $1{:}3$. The four stages use 287,360 updates in total; Appendix B gives target construction, network sizes, and training settings.
